The Cost of Half-Hearted Work

Colossians 3:23–24

Wednesday, November 25, 2026 · 2 min read

Scripture

“Whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord and not for men, knowing that from the Lord you will receive the inheritance as your reward. You are serving the Lord Christ.” — Colossians 3:23–24

Reflection

Most men know the drift: the task in front of you is ordinary, the pay is ordinary, and no one is watching closely. It is easy to give a task half your attention while your mind runs elsewhere—to the next thing, the bigger thing, the thing that might finally matter. Paul writes to a church surrounded by a culture that despised manual labor, and he tells slaves and masters alike to work heartily—literally, “from the soul.” The motive is not the paycheck or the promotion but the Lord Christ, who sees the work no one else notices.

This reframes the hidden hours. The spreadsheet, the repair job, the difficult conversation with a coworker—none of it is wasted when offered to Christ. Half-hearted work is not mainly a productivity problem; it is a vision problem. We forget who we are actually serving. When the audience is the Lord, excellence stops being performance and becomes worship. It also frees us from bitterness when our labor goes unseen. The inheritance Paul mentions is not earned but received—grace undergirding every faithful hour.

Practically, this means doing the next task well, whether or not anyone applauds. It means refusing to cut corners when no one checks. It means working hard without becoming a workaholic, because our identity rests in Christ, not in output. Ask yourself today: who am I working for in this moment? Answer honestly, then offer the task—small or large—to the One who sees.

Prayer

Lord Christ, forgive us for working half-heartedly and for serving applause instead of you. Train our hands and hearts to labor faithfully in the hidden hours, for your glory and not our own. Amen.

One-Line Application

Work today as if Christ were your only audience—because he is.
